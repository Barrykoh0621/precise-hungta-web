How to Choose a Tensile Testing Machine in Malaysia

A practical guide for factories, QA teams and laboratories comparing tensile testing machines, universal testing machines, grips, standards, software reports and calibration support.

Ask for selection help WhatsApp Hung Ta

Step 1

Start with the material and force range

Metal and steel

Often require higher force capacity, suitable grips, elongation measurement and test method alignment such as ASTM E8 or ISO 6892.

Rubber and plastics

Often require lower force range, suitable dumbbell or strip grips, elongation measurement and standards such as ASTM D412 or ISO 37.

Wire, cable and copper

Often require secure gripping, elongation or break-force reporting and careful fixture selection to avoid sample slip.

Step 2

Choose machine type based on use case

Electrical servo tensile machine

Usually suitable for controlled tensile, compression, peel, flexural and bending tests at lower to medium capacity.

Hydraulic universal testing machine

Often suitable for higher capacity applications such as metal, construction materials, structural parts and heavy-duty testing.

Universal testing machine

Useful when the same system needs to support tensile, compression, bending or other material strength tests with different fixtures.

Step 3

Do not ignore grips, software and calibration support

Grips and fixtures

Grip selection can decide whether the test is usable. Share sample dimensions and material before quotation.

Reports and software

Confirm if you need force-extension graphs, Excel export, PDF reports, test method templates or customer-specific report formats.

Service readiness

Ask about installation, training, servicing, spare parts and calibration support before purchase, not only after a machine problem appears.
