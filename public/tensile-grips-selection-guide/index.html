Selecting Tensile Grips for Metal, Rubber and Copper Wire

Machine capacity is only one part of a tensile testing setup. Grip selection also depends on the specimen, test method and machine connection. Use this guide to prepare a clear enquiry before ordering replacement grips or a new testing system.

Start with your specimen

Information to discuss with your supplier, not a final grip specification

Confirm the final fixture with the supplier against your machine and test method. A grip design used on another system is not automatically compatible with yours.

Check compatibility before quotation

  1. Specimen: send dimensions, material and clear photos of the sample and existing grip.
  2. Force range: share the expected load and the rating of your machine and load cell.
  3. Connection: provide the machine model and details of the existing mounting arrangement.
  4. Test method: identify the customer requirement or test standard and the available test space.
  5. Operation: explain how often samples are changed and whether manual, pneumatic or hydraulic actuation is available on the system.

When to ask for a setup review

If samples repeatedly slip, are visibly damaged at the jaws or cannot be positioned consistently, record the issue and contact support. Share photographs, sample dimensions and the current grip model. Calibration checks measurement performance; it does not choose the right fixture or repair damaged jaws.

For machine selection, see our tensile testing machine guide. For measurement enquiries, see testing machine calibration support.
