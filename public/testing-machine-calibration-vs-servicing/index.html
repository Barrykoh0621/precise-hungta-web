Testing Machine Calibration vs Verification and Servicing

Your tensile tester is due for calibration, but the force reading drifts or the specimen slips. Should you book calibration, request repair or review the grips first? Separate the requirement before arranging the work.

Discuss calibration supportPlan machine service

Three different questions about the same machine

Calibration: what does it measure?

Establish the relationship between the machine indication and reference measurements under stated conditions. The measurement results and uncertainty matter; calibration is not the same as adjusting the machine.

Verification: does it meet the requirement?

Check evidence against specified acceptance requirements. Tell the provider which test method, measurement range and acceptance criteria your QA system requires before work is agreed.

Servicing: what needs attention?

Investigate the condition of the equipment, from worn grips and controller faults to mechanical or hydraulic problems. A repair or adjustment is not, by itself, a calibration record.

What to send for common tensile testing machine problems

Force reading drifts or does not return to zero

Send the machine model, load cell capacity, a description of when the drift occurs and any recent overload, repair or controller change. Ask for a condition review before confirming calibration arrangements; a symptom alone does not identify the cause.

Rubber, metal or copper specimens slip in the grips

Share sample dimensions, material, grip photographs and the expected force range. Grip suitability and alignment need review. See the tensile grip selection guide and grips and fixtures catalogue.

The machine works, but the calibration due date is approaching

Send the previous record, machine and load cell identity, working range, required date and factory location. Use our testing machine calibration checklist to prepare a complete enquiry.

A load cell or controller has been replaced

State exactly what was changed and share the service record. Ask what checks and measurement records are required before returning the equipment to its intended testing work.

Do all machines need annual calibration?

There is no universal interval for every machine. Check your customer or regulatory requirements, applicable method, quality system, usage and previous performance. NIST does not recommend one fixed recalibration interval for all measuring instruments. Plan around your own documented requirements.

Clarify the measurement scope before booking

Machine capacity and your normal working range are not interchangeable. A tensile tester may have more than one load cell or measurement function. Identify the force range, tension or compression direction, and whether displacement or extension measurements are part of the request. Confirm the agreed scope, provider and required records before accepting a quotation.

A useful first enquiry

Include: brand and model; serial number; machine and load cell capacities; normal force range; current symptoms; last service and calibration records; factory location; required date; and the test method or customer requirement.
